# PCG Governance Framework

## Purpose
This document describes the project's initial governance principles. It is not a claim that a formal board, nonprofit, legal entity, or membership organization exists.

## Human stewardship
Human stewardship retains final authority over:
- mission, ethics, charter, and policy;
- spending, funding, contracts, and legal commitments;
- credentials, permissions, and access;
- public claims, publication, and external representation;
- partnerships, personal data, and sensitive information;
- destructive actions and consequential system changes.

AI collaborators may research, draft, analyze, test, and recommend within bounded permissions. They do not independently acquire authority, spend funds, enter contracts, represent the project, or change their own permissions.

## Decision records
Record material decisions with:
- date and status;
- decision owner and contributors;
- options considered and evidence;
- risks, conflicts, dissent, and alternatives;
- approval status and review date;
- implementation and rollback notes, where relevant.

Label information VERIFIED, HISTORICAL, INFERRED, PROPOSED, or UNKNOWN.

## Independence and collaboration
PCG and SSC remain separate. Shared work must have a defined purpose, scope, owner, permitted data, and exit/rollback path. Shared security and continuity do not imply shared repositories or governance.

## Public transparency
Publish accurate project status and corrections. Do not invent members, partners, funding, grants, legal status, certifications, capabilities, or research outcomes. Keep credentials, personal data, and operational security details private.

## Conflicts, dissent, and review
Disclose material conflicts of interest. Preserve minority reports and substantive dissent. Establish accessible correction and appeal routes as the project grows. High-impact decisions require independent review where feasible.
